# Gate RR-0016 (revised) — advocate (2026-11-18)

Sound frame: no-RNG combat, owner/controller, supply via variant-B flows, 4 outcomes. Five rejected or simplified alternatives are stronger than the draft.

1. **strong — war termination (I2).** `stalemate = f(advance≈0, losses, time)` is a heuristic. Alternative [Предл.]: bargaining model (Fearon 1995, "war as bargaining"): each side has its own outcome estimate (via `intel_conf`, RR-0017) and a cost of continuing; peace when estimates converge and a bargaining range opens. A Korea-style armistice emerges, and the report explains why ("they think they are winning because they see us badly"). Unconditional "fading" can be derived from the same model. Adopt in I2; formula by simulation-engineer.
2. **strong — air in M2 without radius (question 4).** Fleet-only air power gives a large power air superiority on any theatre; Falklands-type range stops mattering. Alternative [Предл.]: a basing proxy from existing data — only the fleet based in own or allied regions in or next to the theatre, plus carriers from the naval fleet; no `airfield_class` needed, M3 refines. Adopt now; reword question 4.
3. **strong — player lever (J2, J3).** A decision portfolio exists but not the cheapest clear lever: front mode (attack / hold / withdraw) with priority, as front plans and general orders in other grand-strategy games [Предп., from memory]. Add front mode to J2; for a key operation show a forecast and top-3 risks before launch (the J3 core), not in v1.1 — otherwise "key operations" means nothing.
4. **moderate — combat abstraction: edges vs operational theatres.** A theatre layer is overkill (few edges per war at 180–280 regions), but #350 makes the front step-like. Alternative [Предл.]: region state `contested` with a visible `advance_acc` (share on the map) and economic damage from fighting; controller unchanged, so #350/#349 hold. Adopt.
5. **moderate — UN by date in 1945** contradicts drift (#365) and treaties as condition events (#256, G2): if the great war went differently, the UN still appears by date. Alternative: an international organisation as a condition event after a war with ≥ N great powers; the League of Nations via the same template. Also: the world wars 1914–45 (total mobilisation, alliance chains) are absent though in M2 they replace scripts (#336) — a gap. [Orchestrator note: the game starts 1945-10-01 (#311), so 1914–45 wars matter only for alternative starts.]
6. **weak–moderate — AI-vs-AI fidelity (question 3).** ≤ 5 ms allows full simulation of all wars — keep. If levels of detail ever come, the criterion must depend on the war (scale, great-power involvement), not on whether the player is involved; otherwise history changes because the player joined.
7. keep — no separate blockade mechanic (sea gates and cut links suffice, disagreement 2); A3, B2, C2, E2 and no RNG in combat win.

Open (owner, costly consequences): `contested` state under #350; "government in exile" status with #362 and full occupation; UN/League as condition events instead of a 1945 date; world wars 1914–45 — a separate RR or RR-0016 section (Orchestrator).
Source for 1: Fearon J., "Rationalist Explanations for War", International Organization 49(3), 1995 [from memory, not verified in session].
